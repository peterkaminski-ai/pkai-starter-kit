# Viewing Your Files

Your agent's whole world — memory, notes, projects, everything — lives in ordinary Markdown files on your computer. You'll want a comfortable app for reading and editing those files, used side by side with your terminal. Terminal for talking to your agent, a review surface for looking at what it wrote: that's the working setup this kit assumes.

Three review surfaces are recommended. They do different jobs, and plenty of people end up with two of them open.

## Typora (recommended)

[Typora](https://typora.io/) is a one-time $15 purchase, with a free 15-day trial to test it out first.

What makes it right for this: Typora shows Markdown as formatted text **while you type** — headers look like headers, bold looks bold, lists look like lists — with no split view and no raw `##` or `**` syntax cluttering the screen. It opens instantly and does nothing else. That's the whole appeal.

> [!tip]
> Cmd-S (Mac) or Ctrl-S (Windows) saves your file. A small dot or asterisk next to the filename in the title bar means you have unsaved changes — save before closing.

## MarkText (free)

If you'd rather not pay, [MarkText](https://www.marktext.app/) is a free, open-source app in the same live-preview style — no split view, formatted text as you type. It's a bit rougher around the edges than Typora, but it's a solid free option.

## MeetingWords (shared)

[MeetingWords](https://meetingwords.com/) is a shared pad: a Markdown document on the web that several people — and several agents — can read and write at the same time. Your agent can put a document on a pad for you to read on your phone, mark it up, and hand it back; and when you work with other houses, a pad is the room you meet in. It's the one surface here that isn't on your computer, so use it for what's meant to be shared, not for what's meant to stay home.

*Disclosure: Pete Kaminski, who wrote this kit, runs MeetingWords. Recommending it isn't intended as self-dealing — it's the current best understanding of what works for people and agents on one document. V3.0 of this kit uses the hosted service at meetingwords.com; self-hosting and an app version come in V3.1.*

## Already using Obsidian or VS Code?

Keep them. Both read and write the same Markdown files, and if one is already your daily tool there's no reason to switch. This kit stopped *teaching* Obsidian in V3 — it's a bigger, more power-user-oriented tool than a first house needs, and its notion of a "vault" isn't how the kit talks about your house — but nothing here forbids it, and nothing in the kit requires anything but plain files.

## A practical habit

Put your terminal and your review surface side by side on screen — two windows, one view. And remember you can ask your agent to open files for you directly. Try something like:

```
open today's session log in Typora
```

Your agent can do that for you — no need to go hunting through folders yourself. If you're on your phone instead, ask it to put the file on a pad.
