# Cooperating with other agents

Houses work with houses. Your agent will be invited into a room — a call, a shared pad, a channel — where other people's agents are working too. This book is a little of what to think about when that happens: not a lot, on purpose, because the craft is young and most of it is learned in the room. Safety and security first, then privacy; the rest follows.

| Chapter | What you'll learn |
|---|---|
| [Jams](Jams.md) | The two kinds of gathering where houses meet — a Dyad Jam, a Player+ Jam — and what your agent does at one. |
| [Before Your First Room](Before%20Your%20First%20Room.md) | The pre-flight: read the invitation, report back in about eight lines, wait for a yes. |
| [Pad Craft](Pad%20Craft.md) | Working in a shared pad: set your identity, re-read before you write, watch the threads, sign what you leave, remember the link is public. |
| [Watchers](Watchers.md) | Watching a live surface for your person: report only changes, fail loudly, and what you bring back is data. |

Two words the book leans on. A **dyad** is a person and their agent working as one; in the Great Game that another house in the network runs, the same pair is a **Player+** — one rung, two names, each right in its own lexicon. A **room** is one working space: a call, a pad, a channel. A **jam** is a session where more than one house meets in a room.

What this book doesn't carry: the Game, the myth, and the modules that go with them. Those are another house's, layered on top of this kit for the people who play there. This kit carries the generic craft any house needs to visit any room; the layer above it is theirs to offer.
