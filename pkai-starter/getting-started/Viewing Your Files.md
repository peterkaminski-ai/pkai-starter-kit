# Viewing Your Files

Your assistant's whole world — memory, notes, projects, everything — lives in ordinary Markdown files on your computer. You'll want a comfortable app for reading and editing those files, used side by side with your terminal. Terminal for talking to your assistant, editor for looking at what it wrote: that's the working setup this kit assumes.

## Typora (recommended)

[Typora](https://typora.io/) is a one-time $15 purchase, with a free 15-day trial to test it out first.

What makes it right for this: Typora shows Markdown as formatted text **while you type** — headers look like headers, bold looks bold, lists look like lists — with no split view and no raw `##` or `**` syntax cluttering the screen. It opens instantly and does nothing else. That's the whole appeal.

> [!tip]
> Cmd-S (Mac) or Ctrl-S (Windows) saves your file. A small dot or asterisk next to the filename in the title bar means you have unsaved changes — save before closing.

## MarkText (free alternative)

If you'd rather not pay, [MarkText](https://www.marktext.app/) is a free, open-source app in the same live-preview style — no split view, formatted text as you type. It's a bit rougher around the edges than Typora, but it's a solid free option.

## Deskham (in development)

[Deskham](https://peterkaminski.ai/) is a new Markdown viewer and editor being developed by Peter Kaminski, designed specifically for this way of working — a Markdown editor meant to sit alongside an assistant in a terminal. It's still in development; watch [peterkaminski.ai](https://peterkaminski.ai/) for news.

## What about Obsidian?

Obsidian is genuinely powerful, and some people in this community run their entire assistant vault inside it. But it's a bigger, more power-user-oriented tool, and if you're just getting started, it can be more than you need on day one. It's completely fine to use Typora or MarkText instead — nothing in this kit requires Obsidian.

> [!tip]
> If you're curious about Obsidian later, this kit includes a whole `obsidian-reference/` wiki you can explore whenever you're ready.

## A practical habit

Put your terminal and your Markdown editor side by side on screen — two windows, one view. And remember you can ask your assistant to open files for you directly. Try something like:

```
open today's session log in Typora
```

Your assistant can do that for you — no need to go hunting through folders yourself.
